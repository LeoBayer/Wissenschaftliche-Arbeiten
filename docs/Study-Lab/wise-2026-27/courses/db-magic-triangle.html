Strategy and business models

Four questions, not three isolated corners

The Magic Triangle places Who? at the center: the target customer segment. What? concerns what is offered to that customer, the value proposition. How? concerns how that proposition is created, the value chain. Value? concerns how revenue is created, the revenue model. The last label does not simply mean “customer satisfaction”: the graphic explicitly links it to revenue creation.

Although the name says triangle, the central customer question matters as much as the corners. A different target segment can change the required offer, delivery arrangement and payment logic. The diagram below preserves the four questions and their explanatory labels.

Use the shape to investigate consistency, not as a causal proof that any combination will succeed. The lecture introduces this framework with an emphasis on innovation paths and then shows patterns and development paths. Historical pattern illustrations can inspire alternatives; they do not certify a new venture or supply measured probabilities of success. No claim that a particular number of changed dimensions is required is inferred from the diagram alone.

Worked redesign: a hypothetical tool business

Start with selling durable tools to occasional household users. Who: households. What: ownership and access when needed. How: procure, stock and sell tools. Value: sales revenue per tool. Consider a rental alternative: the household receives temporary access rather than ownership, so availability, collection, return inspection and maintenance become necessary parts of delivery. Revenue now depends on rental days, not sales units.

For an own simplified rental calculation, assume €20 revenue and €8 variable cost per paid rental day, plus €2,400/month fixed cost including the assumed monthly cost of the tool stock. At 250 paid rental days/month, revenue = €5,000 and contribution = 250 × €12 = €3,000; operating result = €600/month. Break-even is 200 paid rental days/month. A rental day is a unit of use, not a distinct customer; 250 days could be spread across fewer customers and multiple tools.

This is not a financial comparison against selling: no sales-case costs or volumes are supplied. Nor does the calculation show liquidity or investment return. Availability, tool damage and demand are material hypotheses. A revenue redesign that removes the ability to fulfill the promise is not coherent innovation.

Understanding pathway

These are own exercises, not official assignments. Write your attempt before opening a hint or solution. Assess your reasoning with the explicit criteria; keyword matching is not evidence of understanding.

1. Reconstruct the four questions

Your attempt: Explain Who, What, How and Value using the rental case. Why does “Value means customers like it” miss the diagram’s distinction?

Optional hint

Separate the offered benefit from the way revenue is created.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Who: occasional household users. What: temporary access to a suitable tool. How: stock, booking, handover, returns and maintenance. Value: revenue per paid rental day. Customer benefit belongs to the proposition; the Value question in this graphic asks about revenue creation.

Assessment: Who/What (1); How (1); Value (1); benefit/revenue distinction (1).

2. Calculate a changed utilization level

Your attempt: At 180 paid rental days/month, calculate revenue, contribution and operating result. How many paid rental days produce €1,200 monthly result under the original assumptions?

Optional hint

Use the €12 margin for the result equation.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Revenue = 180 × €20 = €3,600/month. Contribution = 180 × €12 = €2,160/month. Result = €2,160 − €2,400 = −€240/month. Target equation: 12q − 2,400 = 1,200, so q = 300 paid rental days/month. Required use is not proven demand and may exceed available stock-day capacity.

Assessment: €3,600; €2,160; −€240; equation/300 days; demand/capacity interpretation: 1 point each (5).

3. Diagnose a superficial redesign

Your attempt: “We changed the price label from sale to rental, so the old delivery chain can remain untouched. A historical pattern proves profitability.” Correct the reasoning.

Optional hint

What must happen after a rented tool is returned?

Complete worked answer and criteria

Rental requires continuing stock ownership, availability planning, returns, inspection and maintenance rather than just a completed sale. Price labeling alone does not create those activities or resources. A pattern suggests a possible model configuration; profitability still depends on utilization, costs, customer willingness to pay and alternatives. Test a small stock with measured rental days, damage and turnaround time.

Assessment: Delivery changes; label/capability distinction; pattern/proof distinction; concrete utilization/cost test: 1 point each (4).

4. Transfer to professional customers

Your attempt: Professional crews require guaranteed next-day replacement. Price becomes €26 per paid day, variable cost €12 and fixed cost €4,000/month. At 300 paid days, calculate the result and compare with the household case at the same use. Find the professional model’s minimum whole paid days for positive result. Explain the nonfinancial dependency.

Optional hint

The new segment alters both the service promise and the cost base.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Professional margin = €14/day; result = 300 × €14 − €4,000 = €200/month. Household result at 300 days = 300 × €12 − €2,400 = €1,200/month. The professional case is €1,000 lower despite higher price. Positive professional result requires q > 4,000/14 = 285.71, hence at least 286 paid days/month. Replacement stock and logistics must meet the guarantee; the comparison assumes equal use, not equal demand, service risk or investment.

Assessment: €14/€200; household €1,200; €1,000 difference; inequality/286 days; service-capacity and comparison limit: 1 point each (5).

Error reflection and delayed retrieval

Maximum 18 self-assessed points (4 + 5 + 4 + 5). Record an error from your first attempt, explain its cause and write a corrected reasoning step. A final number without its reasoning earns no reasoning credit. Tomorrow: reconstruct the model and solve task 2 unaided. In one week: repeat the professional case at 280 paid days and explain why a higher price can coexist with a worse result. Check units and interpret the changed assumptions.
