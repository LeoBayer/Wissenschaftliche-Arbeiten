Blockchain beyond cryptocurrency

Digital Business · Course 95696 · WiSe 2026/27

Begin with the application, not the labelUnderstand and applySelf-check (SC)Source and limits

Digital Business overview

Learning goals

Identify the coordination problem in a non-currency application and distinguish a token’s identity from the rights or real-world facts associated with it.

Begin with the application, not the label

Blockchain application scenarios include vaccine passports, supply-chain traceability and transparency, music-industry platforms such as Audius, and art-related non-fungible tokens. These are application illustrations, not comparative evidence that blockchain is always the best architecture. The slides offer much less implementation detail here than in the foundations section.

To turn this list into an explanation, apply the distinction between integrity, authorization and agreement: What is recorded, which parties need to agree, and what remains outside the record?

Vaccine passport: Which issuer’s statement is checked? A consistent record cannot independently establish the medical event.

Supply chain: Which handover or product event is recorded? A false physical-world input can still be recorded consistently.

Music platform: Which participants exchange or coordinate value? A platform name alone does not establish contractual rights or governance rules.

Art-related NFT: Which unique token changes hands? Token identity alone does not establish copyright or ownership of a physical artwork.

The questions and boundaries are original analytical prompts, not claims that the cited projects implemented particular features or failed in these ways. They help avoid answering a design question with a product name.

Original learning example: tracing a food shipment

Imagine three firms recording successive handovers of one shipment. A shared history could support checking which handover was entered and by whom. If someone attaches the wrong product identifier at the first handover, later agreement on that identifier does not repair the initial mistake.

A reasoned proposal must therefore specify input controls as well as record integrity. It should also ask whether participants actually need a shared agreement mechanism or whether a conventional database under an accepted operator would suffice. This is a design comparison derived from the lecture’s “cost of trust” framing, not evidence that one option is superior.

Fungible does not mean “digital”; non-fungible does not mean “copyright”

Bitcoin units are interchangeable, whereas other applications may require units to be distinguished. Fungibility concerns interchangeability within the relevant class; non-fungibility concerns the need to identify a particular unit. Both can involve digital records.

Original learning example: Ten interchangeable admission credits differ from a token identifying one numbered collectible. Identifying that collectible is still a different question from deciding what legal permissions the buyer receives. The lecture’s art-sale example illustrates a transaction; it does not by itself document a general transfer of copyright.

What the section cannot settle

The linked examples are historical teaching material. This page makes no claim that the projects are currently available, legally unchanged or commercially successful. Where the slides provide only a heading and external links, the implementation is not specified, rather than filled in with invented details.

Understanding pathway

These are own exercises, not official assignment tasks. Write your attempt before opening a hint or answer. Assess reasoning, units, interpretation and assumptions using the criteria; keyword matching does not demonstrate understanding.

1. Rebuild an application argument

Your attempt: For a shipment handover and a numbered art token, identify what is recorded, who needs to coordinate and one fact the record cannot establish alone. Explain fungibility without equating it with being digital.

Optional hint

A record identity is not automatically a physical fact or a bundle of rights.

Complete worked answer and criteria

A shipment history records attributed handover claims among supply-chain participants, but cannot alone prove correct physical labeling. A numbered token identifies a particular unit and transfer between participants; the token record alone does not establish the associated copyright or physical ownership. Fungibility concerns interchangeability within a class; both interchangeable credits and individually identified tokens can be digital. Exact implementations and rights require separate evidence and are not supplied by these examples.

Assessment: Shipment record/actors/limit, token record/actors/limit, interchangeability distinction, implementation/rights boundary: 1 point each (4).

2. Calculate remaining input errors

Your attempt: Own shipment-control case: 1,000 records contain 30 wrong product identifiers before review. A manual input check finds and corrects 24 of these; assume no new errors and no other incorrect inputs. All entries subsequently pass integrity and agreement checks. Calculate remaining errors, remaining error share and the share of original errors corrected.

Optional hint

Use 1,000 for the record error share but 30 for correction coverage.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Remaining errors: 30 − 24 = 6. Remaining error share: 6/1,000 = 0.6%. Original-error correction coverage: 24/30 = 80%. These are different denominators and neither proves perfect input. Successful integrity and agreement checks preserve agreement about the entered identifiers; they do not reduce the 6 physical-label errors by themselves. The assumed review identifies known errors perfectly; real review performance would need validation.

Assessment: 6 remaining, 0.6% with denominator, 80% with denominator, record/input distinction, review assumption: 1 point each (5).

3. Diagnose two application shortcuts

Your attempt: A proposal says: “A famous music platform uses blockchain, therefore our supply chain needs it. Selling a unique token automatically gives the buyer every right to the artwork.” Repair both claims and propose a comparison for the supply-chain decision.

Optional hint

A named example is not a requirements analysis; uniqueness is not a complete rights description.

Complete worked answer and criteria

A platform example establishes neither the same coordination problem nor superiority for a supply chain. Compare a shared-record architecture with a conventional database operated by an accepted party, using explicit needs for agreement, governance, input quality and operating effort. A unique token identifies a unit but does not specify every associated permission. Investigate the actual arrangement instead of inferring rights from the token label; this is an analytical boundary, not legal advice about any named sale.

Assessment: No inference across use cases, explicit alternative, relevant comparison criteria, token/rights distinction: 1 point each (4).

4. Transfer to an accepted operator

Your attempt: The three shipment firms now all accept one neutral database operator and an audit process. They still need reliable product identifiers. Does blockchain become logically necessary? Explain what changes in the architecture comparison, what remains unchanged and what evidence could change your answer again.

Optional hint

Remove the supposed coordination need before reusing the original recommendation.

Complete worked answer and criteria

No logical necessity follows. An accepted operator makes a conventional shared database a plausible comparator; evaluate access, auditability, failure handling and cost rather than assuming the label decides. Incorrect physical input remains possible in either architecture, so identifier validation is still needed. If firms no longer accept the operator or have new agreement/governance requirements, reassess how each option meets them. That change does not automatically prove blockchain best: implementation, input controls and costs still require evidence. These are own design prompts, not implementation facts about the historical projects.

Assessment: No necessity, changed accepted-operator assumption, unchanged input problem, explicit reassessment trigger, conditional comparison with evidence: 1 point each (5).

Error reflection and delayed retrieval

Maximum 18 self-assessed points (4 + 5 + 4 + 5). Record one incorrect denominator, missing cost or unsupported causal link from your first attempt and correct its explanation. A correct label or final number without reasoning earns no reasoning credit. Tomorrow: reconstruct tasks 1 and 2 unaided. In one week: repeat the transfer with only 20 of the 30 incorrect inputs are found; recalculate the remaining error share.
