OSINT: information, coordination and dual use

IT-driven Innovation · Course 96437 · WiSe 2026/27

MechanismWorked exampleUnderstand and applySelf-checkSources

Back to IT-driven Innovation

Explain the mechanism

Open-source intelligence (OSINT) turns publicly available information into potentially actionable intelligence. Access to data is only one part of the process: interpretation, combination and verification matter. Crowdsourcing distributes parts of a task across many participants; a coordination platform can organize their contributions without guaranteeing that every clue is correct.

In the missing-person case, participants in Trace Labs events collect public clues, Trace Labs vets information, and findings are forwarded to law enforcement. These stages have different roles. Engagement through gamified events can mobilize effort; vetting is a separate quality-control step. A large volume of contributions does not eliminate that step.

Layer What it enables for searchers What it does not establish
OSINT tools Access information; search names, images and similarities; identify locations; consider changes over time Correct identity, relevance or current location
Coordination platform Form search parties; share and aggregate clues; observe co-searcher activity Truth of an aggregated claim or permission to expose a person publicly
Review and handover process Check findings before forwarding them to law enforcement A guaranteed resolution of a case

The same tools can support beneficial searches or harmful exposure and manipulation. This is a relational account: ask what an artifact allows which actor to do, with which goals and consequences. “Publicly accessible” describes availability; it does not demonstrate accuracy, harmlessness or an ethical justification for every reuse. The aim here is to analyse these mechanisms, not to conduct investigations into real individuals.

Worked example

Own fictional review exercise; do not collect personal data. A team submits 60 clue records. Review finds that 15 duplicate existing records. Of the remaining 45, 9 are unsupported. There are therefore 36 distinct supported records under these simplifying assumptions. Relative to all submissions, that is 36/60 = 60%; relative to distinct records, it is 36/45 = 80%. Neither percentage is the probability of finding a missing person.

Suppose a dashboard rewards only raw submissions. Repeated copies can raise the visible count without adding distinct evidence. The appropriate response is not simply “remove the platform”: separate submission, deduplication and verification metrics, and retain the review stage before handover. A supported record can still be irrelevant to resolving a case. Multiple independent contributions may corroborate a claim, but copied versions of the same source do not create independent corroboration.

Understanding pathway

These are own exercises, not official assignment or examination questions. Write your attempt before opening a hint. Use the complete solution and concrete criteria to assess your reasoning; no keyword matching is used. A correct number without its requested reasoning does not earn reasoning credit.

1. Separate tool from platform

Your attempt: From memory, give two different actions enabled by OSINT tools and two by the coordination platform. Explain why sharing a clue is not verifying it.

Optional hint

Distinguish finding content from organizing joint work.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Tools can support searching names/images and identifying locations from public material. The platform can support forming teams and sharing or aggregating findings. Sharing changes who can see a claim; verification evaluates its accuracy and relevance. Many repetitions of one unverified claim do not by themselves verify it.

Assessment: Two tool actions (1), two coordination actions (1), sharing/verification distinction (1), repetition limitation (1): 4 points.

2. Audit contribution metrics

Your attempt: Own fictional dataset: 80 submissions include 20 duplicates. Of the remaining distinct records, 15 are unsupported. Compute supported distinct records, their share of all submissions and their share of distinct records. Explain the denominator difference.

Optional hint

Remove duplicates first; the 15 unsupported records are drawn from the remainder.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Distinct records: 80 − 20 = 60. Supported distinct records: 60 − 15 = 45. Share of all submissions: 45/80 = 56.25%. Share of distinct records: 45/60 = 75%. The former measures supported distinct output relative to submission volume; the latter measures support among unique records. Neither measures cases solved or causal effectiveness.

Assessment: 60 distinct, 45 supported, 56.25%, 75%, denominator interpretation and outcome limit: 1 point each (5).

3. Find two errors in a dashboard claim

Your attempt: “Our platform has 80 submissions, so we have 80 independent verified facts. Everything is public, so forwarding it to any audience cannot cause harm.” Correct both arguments and suggest a process control for each.

Optional hint

Quality and audience are separate issues.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Submissions can duplicate each other or remain unsupported. Keep provenance, deduplicate and review before counting verified distinct findings. Public availability does not make every aggregation or redistribution harmless: audience, purpose and possible exposure matter. Use a bounded review/handover process rather than treating public broadcasting as equivalent to vetted handover. These are analytical controls, not additional claims about the platform’s actual policies.

Assessment: Evidence error, quality control, public/harmlessness error, audience-sensitive control: 1 point each (4).

4. Transfer when coordination disappears

Your attempt: The search tools are unchanged, but private team workspaces are removed and every participant sees only their own records. Explain two likely coordination losses, propose an alternative workflow and state an uncertainty.

Optional hint

Do not confuse retained access to information with retained collective action.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Individual search remains possible, but co-searchers lose shared aggregation and visibility of others’ work; duplicate effort or missed connections may increase. A moderated handover queue with a coordinator could consolidate records and assign non-overlapping work, while preserving review and a bounded audience. This is a proposed replacement, not evidence that it will match the old platform. Evaluate duplicate rates, review workload and quality; private work alone does not guarantee less or more overall harm.

Assessment: Retained individual capability, two coordination losses, feasible reviewed alternative, uncertainty plus evaluation: 1 point each (5).

Error reflection and delayed retrieval

Maximum 18 self-assessed points (4 + 5 + 4 + 5). Record whether your first answer confused volume, uniqueness, support or social outcome. Tomorrow, reconstruct the tool/platform distinction and recompute both percentages. In one week, analyse a platform with sharing but no review stage; identify what your original controls no longer cover. Distinguish a corrected answer from the reason your first attempt failed.
