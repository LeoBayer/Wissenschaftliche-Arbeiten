Channels and relationships: design the whole customer journey

Digital Business · Course 95696 · WiSe 2026/27

Mechanism and distinctions

A channel explains how a company communicates with, reaches and delivers value to a segment. It includes more than advertising or checkout. The five phases are awareness, evaluation, purchase, delivery and after sales. A useful analysis asks what the customer needs at each phase and where information or responsibility is lost between them.

Channel types describe routes such as sales force, web sales, own stores, partner stores and wholesalers. Channel phases describe functions over the transaction. These are not interchangeable categories. Web sales can support purchase while another route performs physical delivery. Owning a channel also differs from reaching customers through a partner.

Read the highlighted columns below horizontally for the phases, then the left column vertically for channel types. The focused original diagram preserves both dimensions and the five explanatory questions. On a narrow screen, scroll the diagram internally; the explanation remains normal-width.

The diagram is a checklist, not evidence that every customer follows a single linear path. Returning customers may skip evaluation; a service problem can trigger renewed evaluation. Merely adding a mobile app does not integrate channels. An online promise must agree with the store’s stock, order and return processes; benefits from integration are hypotheses to evaluate, not guaranteed effects of channel count.

A customer relationship instead describes the interaction established with a segment and its purpose: acquisition, retention or upselling. Personal assistance provides human help; dedicated assistance assigns a representative to a particular client. Self-service supplies means to help oneself. Automated service adds processes such as customer-specific recommendations. Communities support exchanges among users; co-creation involves customers in producing value, such as reviews or design contributions. The same website can carry different relationship designs.

Retention should not be reduced to satisfaction: switching costs can also make leaving difficult. Likewise a community does not ensure correct advice or remove moderation costs. Channel convenience, relationship quality and the customer’s ability to leave require separate evaluation.

Worked example

Own hypothetical repair-service journey. A customer discovers a mobile bicycle repair through a search ad (awareness), compares a repair checklist (evaluation), books and pays online (purchase), receives an on-site repair (delivery), and uses a help portal for follow-up (after sales). The portal is a channel; self-service with human escalation is the relationship design. A named adviser for each business fleet would be dedicated assistance rather than simply another channel.

Assume 200 completed jobs/month and 30% require after-sales support. Under full personal assistance, each support case takes 12 paid minutes. At €30 per paid support hour, cost is 200 × 0.30 × 12/60 × €30 = €360/month.

A self-service portal is assumed to resolve half of those 60 cases without personal help. The remaining 30 still take 12 minutes each. Personal support falls to €180/month; portal operation costs €100/month, giving €280/month total, a conditional saving of €80/month. The portal does not erase support demand; its success rate and unresolved-customer outcomes require measurement. These are own cost assumptions, not an original lecture calculation.

Understanding pathway

Own exercises, not official assignments. Write your attempt before opening a hint or solution. Check reasoning, units and interpretation using the criteria; a final number alone earns no reasoning credit. Open answers are self-assessed, not validated by keyword matching.

1. Map a journey from memory

Your attempt: Name the five phases and give a repair-service touchpoint for each. Explain why self-service is not a sixth phase.

Optional hint

Separate the purpose of a touchpoint from the way the interaction is organized.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Awareness: search ad; evaluation: diagnostic checklist; purchase: booking/payment; delivery: on-site repair; after sales: help portal. Self-service describes how a relationship is organized and can apply at several phases; it is not a transaction phase. A website can support several phases rather than belonging to only one.

Assessment: All five phases (2 points); fitting touchpoints (1); relationship/phase distinction (1): 4 points.

2. Calculate a different resolution rate

Your attempt: Use the worked case, but only 25% of the 60 support cases are fully resolved by the portal. Compute remaining personal cases, paid hours, total support cost and difference from full personal assistance.

Optional hint

Resolved cases are 15, not 25% of all 200 jobs.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Portal resolves 60 × 0.25 = 15 cases; 45 need personal help. Paid time = 45 × 12/60 = 9 hours/month. Personal cost = 9 × €30 = €270/month; with portal operation total = €370/month, €10 more than €360 full assistance. Under unchanged case time and portal cost, a lower resolution rate can overturn the saving.

Assessment: 45 cases; 9 hours; €270; €370/+€10; correct denominator and interpretation: 1 point each, 5 points.

3. Find two design errors

Your attempt: A retailer says opening a second app proves omnichannel integration, and that the new app is a dedicated personal relationship. Diagnose both claims and propose a concrete integration check.

Optional hint

Channel count does not say whether order data connect or a person is assigned.

Complete worked answer and criteria

A second app adds a touchpoint, not evidence of integrated stock/order/return handling. Dedicated personal assistance requires an assigned human representative, not merely an interface. Test whether a customer can purchase online and receive the promised item/support in-store with consistent order information and clear responsibility.

Assessment: Count/integration error; app/relationship error; assigned-person criterion; concrete cross-channel test: 1 point each, 4 points.

4. Transfer to difficult unresolved cases

Your attempt: The portal resolves 50% of 60 cases, but remaining cases now average 20 minutes. Portal cost stays €100/month and staff cost €30/hour. Compute total cost and the strict resolution-rate threshold for a saving versus €360, assuming unresolved cases stay at 20 minutes. Explain one quality risk.

Optional hint

One unresolved case now costs €10; use 60(1 − r).

Complete worked answer and criteria

Remaining 30 cases require 10 hours, costing €300; total = €400/month, €40 above full assistance. With resolution fraction r, cost = 60(1 − r) × €10 + €100 = €700 − €600r. Saving needs 700 − 600r < 360, so r > 340/600 ≈ 56.67%. Equality gives no saving. Difficult cases may be delayed or abandoned; measure resolution quality and customer outcomes, not only reduced human contacts. The threshold assumes fixed difficulty/cost and no extra follow-up.

Assessment: €400; +€40; correct cost equation; strict threshold; quality risk and fixed-assumption boundary: 1 point each, 5 points.

Error reflection and delayed retrieval

Maximum 18 self-assessed points (4 + 5 + 4 + 5). Record an error from your first attempt, its cause and the corrected reasoning. Distinguish a denominator error, a unit error and an unjustified causal claim. Tomorrow, map a new purchase through all five phases and repeat the 25% calculation. In one week, compare self-service with community support, including moderation and unresolved-case costs.
