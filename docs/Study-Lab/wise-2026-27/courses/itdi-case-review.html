Content, narrative and production checks

Review in the right order

Content, structure and quality jointly matter. Start with the argument: can a viewer understand the technology, what it enables, and why the claimed impacts are plausible? Research the case thoroughly and draft the narrative in detail before spending scarce production time on effects.

The following review sequence is an own working method, not an official marking rubric:

  • Content: separate supported claims from assumptions; keep the link between functionality and impact visible.
  • Structure: give every scene a role in the argument. Remove repetition before removing important conditions.
  • Quality: check whether speech, text and relevant visuals are intelligible in the final exported video.
  • Compliance: measure the actual exported runtime against the hard 120-second limit and check the participation decision separately.

Production style is flexible: a camera recording or an animated short can be appropriate. Freedom of style does not remove the requirement for quality. No particular software purchase is required by this introduction.

A timing example, not a prescribed template

An illustrative plan allocates 15 seconds to problem and actor, 35 to functionality and action possibility, 40 to potential impact and limitations, and 20 to the conclusion. That totals 110 seconds, leaving 10 seconds of headroom within 120.

This allocation is a planning example, not an official section budget or a requirement to use exactly 110 seconds. Titles, transitions, pauses and credits all occupy runtime in the export; adding a 15-second ending to a 110-second cut produces 125 seconds. Check the final file rather than relying on the spoken script alone.

For example, a transcription case could replace a decorative introduction with a short before/after search demonstration, then explain the correction-effort condition. The purpose is to make the argument understandable, not to claim that a demonstration establishes a general productivity effect.

Understanding pathway

These are own exercises, not official assignment tasks. Write your attempt before opening a hint or answer. Assess reasoning, units, interpretation and assumptions using the criteria; keyword matching does not demonstrate understanding.

1. Reconstruct a review decision

Your attempt: Without the page, explain why an impressive animation can still be a weak submission. Give one observable check each for content, structure and quality, and distinguish a grading criterion from your own review sequence.

Optional hint

Ask what the viewer learns, how the causal argument unfolds and whether it is intelligible.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Content: verify that the technology’s functionality supports the stated action possibility and that impact conditions are explicit. Structure: check that the scenes develop that argument rather than repeat claims. Quality: listen to the exported speech and inspect text legibility at intended playback size. Content, structure and quality are assessment criteria; this particular order of checks is our working method, not an official point allocation. Effects cannot substitute for a researched argument.

Assessment: One concrete check for each criterion plus official/own distinction: 1 point each (4).

2. Budget the exported runtime

Your attempt: Own case: the current export is 112 seconds, including all existing titles. New credits add 9 seconds and transitions add 4. You can remove 7 seconds of repeated introduction. Calculate the final runtime and remaining headroom. Explain what must be measured before submission.

Optional hint

Count every added and removed second once; 120 is a ceiling, not a target.

Complete worked answer and criteria

112 + 9 + 4 − 7 = 118 seconds. Headroom is 120 − 118 = 2 seconds. Without the cut, 125 seconds would exceed the limit by 5. Export and measure the actual final file, including credits and transitions; a script estimate alone is insufficient. The 2-second margin is an arithmetic result, not a guarantee that later export changes preserve compliance.

Assessment: 125-second subtotal, subtraction/118, 2 seconds headroom, units/ceiling, actual export measurement: 1 point each (5).

3. Diagnose two review shortcuts

Your attempt: A teammate says: “Our demo saves 30 seconds, so it proves a general productivity gain. Tournament participation also determines our grade.” Correct both claims and name a useful next check.

Optional hint

A demonstration and an estimated population effect need different evidence.

Complete worked answer and criteria

One demonstration can illustrate a mechanism but does not establish a general effect: correction effort, case variation and workflow boundaries may change the net result. Check sources and explicitly state the conditions of the impact claim. Entering the tournament is a separate decision; grades do not depend on entering it. Neither a prize nor visual polish repairs an unsupported causal claim.

Assessment: Demo/evidence distinction, plausible limiting condition, concrete verification, separate tournament decision: 1 point each (4).

4. Transfer when a necessary condition needs time

Your attempt: Use the 118-second cut above. A missing 8-second explanation of correction effort is essential to qualify the impact claim. Only 3 seconds of decorative effects can be removed. Can the team comply by these edits alone? Propose a reasoned revision without deleting the condition.

Optional hint

Recompute duration before recommending a cosmetic cut.

Complete worked answer and criteria

118 + 8 − 3 = 123 seconds: 3 seconds over the hard limit. Those edits alone cannot comply. Remove or compress at least 3 more seconds of repetition while preserving intelligibility, or redesign the scene so the condition is communicated within a shorter but clear argument. A 3-second further cut reaches exactly 120; extra margin may be useful but is not an official requirement. Re-export, measure and review meaning again.

Assessment: 123 seconds, 3-second excess, preserve condition, feasible additional edit, re-export and clarity check: 1 point each (5).

Error reflection and delayed retrieval

Maximum 18 self-assessed points (4 + 5 + 4 + 5). Record one incorrect denominator, missing cost or unsupported causal link from your first attempt and correct its explanation. A correct label or final number without reasoning earns no reasoning credit. Tomorrow: reconstruct tasks 1 and 2 unaided. In one week: repeat the transfer with a 105-second base cut; distinguish arithmetic headroom from argument quality.

Final-cut self-check

A 110-second cut receives a further 15-second ending. Which judgement is correct?

Show explanation

Answer b. The total is 110 + 15 = 125 seconds. The 110-second plan is our example, not a source requirement. Additional visual material does not create an exemption from the duration limit.
