STOF: align service, technology, organization and finance

Digital Business · Course 95696 · WiSe 2026/27

MechanismWorked exampleUnderstand and applySelf-checkSources

Strategy and business models

Four domains that constrain one another

STOF separates Service, Technology, Organization and Financial domains. Service concerns the value proposition and market segment. Technology concerns the functionality required to provide it. Organization concerns the structure of the value network: which actors contribute and how delivery depends on them. Finance concerns the cost structure and profit potential. A functioning technology is only one part of this model.

The diagram connects domains in both directions and distinguishes customer value from network value. Market developments, technological developments and changes in legislation surround the model. They can force reconsideration of a previously coherent arrangement; they are not a fifth internal domain.

Customer value includes ease of use, costs and experience. Network value includes revenues and access to critical resources. They may support each other, but a pleasant experience does not automatically compensate a necessary partner. Equally, high platform revenue does not demonstrate that users receive a useful service. The arrows show interdependence, not measured coefficients or guaranteed profit.

Worked alignment: a hypothetical booking service

An own teaching case connects households to local energy advisers. The service promises a useful appointment within two working days. Technology supplies availability matching and notifications. The organizational arrangement requires independent advisers to publish real slots and accept assignments. The financial design must compensate those advisers and cover support. Showing an attractive calendar is insufficient if nobody can fulfill appointments.

Assume a €50 fee per completed appointment, €30 adviser payment, €5 processing/support cost and €1,200 monthly fixed cost. At 100 completions, revenue is €5,000; variable cost is €3,500; contribution is €1,500; operating result is €300/month. The equality volume is 1,200/15 = 80 completed appointments/month. This simplified operating calculation excludes taxes, setup and financing and is not evidence of demand or adviser participation.

A two-day promise therefore creates linked tests: measure completion time and usefulness (service/customer value), matching reliability (technology), adviser acceptance and capacity (organization), and contribution after all required payments (finance/network viability). A canceled booking is not a completed appointment. The model helps locate inconsistencies; it does not predict their resolution without evidence.

Understanding pathway

These are own exercises, not official assignments. Write your attempt before opening a hint or solution. Assess your reasoning with the explicit criteria; keyword matching is not evidence of understanding.

1. Reconstruct the four domains

Your attempt: Explain each STOF domain for the adviser case and why “the matching algorithm works” is not a sufficient acceptance criterion.

Optional hint

Trace the promise from a customer outcome to delivery and payment.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Service: useful advice within two working days for households. Technology: matching and notification functionality. Organization: advisers must supply slots and deliver appointments. Finance: fees must cover adviser payments, support and fixed costs. A working algorithm leaves participation, completion quality and economics unproven.

Assessment: Correct service and technology (1); organization (1); finance (1); insufficiency explained (1).

2. Apply the financial domain

Your attempt: An adviser requires €34 instead of €30 per completion. All other assumptions stay unchanged. Compute the result at 100 completions and the minimum whole completion volume for positive operating result.

Optional hint

Only adviser payment changes: margin becomes €50 − €34 − €5.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Contribution is €11/completion. At 100, contribution = €1,100/month and result = €1,100 − €1,200 = −€100/month. Positive result requires 11q > 1,200, hence q > 109.09 and at least 110 whole completed appointments/month. Verify capacity and willingness to pay before treating that requirement as feasible.

Assessment: €11; €1,100; −€100; inequality/110 completions; feasibility boundary: 1 point each (5).

3. Diagnose a domain mix-up

Your attempt: “Customer satisfaction is high, so every adviser must earn enough. A new data-retention rule affects only the finance box.” Correct both claims and name an affected connection.

Optional hint

Customer value and partner viability differ; a rule can change functionality and roles.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Satisfaction does not reveal adviser revenue, costs or willingness to participate. Network value and customer value require separate evidence. A hypothetical data-retention rule can alter stored data and matching functionality, organizational responsibilities, service continuity and compliance cost. For example, reducing usable data may affect matching and the two-day promise. No specific real legal obligation is asserted here.

Assessment: Value distinction; partner evidence; multi-domain regulatory effect; concrete connection: 1 point each (4).

4. Transfer to a reliability upgrade

Your attempt: An upgrade adds €400/month fixed cost, keeps the original €15 contribution and raises feasible completions from 100 to 130. Compare original and upgraded result. Find the minimum whole additional completions needed to improve on the original result.

Optional hint

Compare incremental contribution with incremental fixed cost, not revenue alone.

Complete worked answer and criteria

Original: 100 × €15 − €1,200 = €300/month. Upgraded: 130 × €15 − €1,600 = €350/month, an improvement of €50. Improvement requires 15Δq > 400, so Δq > 26.67: at least 27 additional completions, assuming no other changes. Technical capacity for 130 does not prove 130 paying completions; customer demand and adviser participation must support the change.

Assessment: €300; €350/+€50; incremental equation; 27 additional completions; capacity/demand distinction: 1 point each (5).

Error reflection and delayed retrieval

Maximum 18 self-assessed points (4 + 5 + 4 + 5). Record an error from your first attempt, explain its cause and write a corrected reasoning step. A final number without its reasoning earns no reasoning credit. Tomorrow: reconstruct the model and solve task 2 unaided. In one week: repeat the upgrade comparison with only 120 feasible completions and explain which domains need revision. Check units and interpret the changed assumptions.
